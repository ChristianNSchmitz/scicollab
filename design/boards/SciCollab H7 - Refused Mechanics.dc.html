BATCH H · BOARD 7 · DECISION BOARD

The eight mechanics — the analysis

SUPERSEDED · ALL EIGHT NOW BUILT
The founders' call: keep all eight and implement them properly, on the argument that engagement aimed at replication and reuse is a genuine improvement at the level of the field, not a retention trick. That is right, and this board's three "refuse" verdicts are now historical. Each mechanic ships with the mitigation drawn out below — see H8, all eight in place. This board is kept because the mocks are the reference for what the naive version looks like, and that is what a future proposal has to be compared against.

Each one is drawn here as it would actually look if built naively — because "we decided against it" is not an argument, and a founder cannot judge a refusal they cannot see. The original reading was three refusals, three already built in a safer form, two under-read and worth building. The refusals turned out to be arguments for redesign, not omission: every one of the three has a version whose incentive points at a verifiable act, and those are what H8 builds.

OF THE 8 IS A PROCESS,
not a screen · K22
The test applied to each: does the mechanic reward the behaviour we want, or a proxy for it? A leaderboard rewards output, which is a proxy for contribution. A replication challenge rewards replication, which is the thing. Where the mechanic and the goal coincide, build it. Where the mechanic rewards a proxy, no amount of tuning fixes it — the proxy is the product.
K10 Invite and referral programme REFUSE
AS IT WOULD LOOK
Invite your 27 co-authors
detected from your imported publications
9 / 27
Invite 18 more to unlock Connector and 500 bonus reputation.
Invite all 27Choose individually
THE CASE FOR
The workbook calls this "the strongest structural growth loop available to a research network". It is not a small thing to give up.
THE INCENTIVE IT CREATES
Reputation for invitations sent. That is a bounty on emailing people who did not ask — and ResearchGate's automated invitations attracted sustained criticism precisely here.
WHAT WE BUILT INSTEAD
Co-author detection with a hard frequency cap, per-person selection, an explicit consent statement and no bulk-blast button — and no reward of any kind for sending one. The growth loop stays; the bounty on it does not.

THE CASE FOR
One click to give, visible to receive, and a strong reciprocity pull. It also fills the skills taxonomy that Fellow-Finder searches.
THE INCENTIVE IT CREATES
Endorse-me-and-I-will-endorse-you. The workbook's phrase is reciprocal inflation, and the endpoint is a number that measures sociability, not skill.
WHAT WE BUILT INSTEAD
Fellow-Finder's evidence column: 7 method cards, 2 datasets, 14 accepted answers, 40 logged instrument sessions. Nobody can endorse their way into it, and it needs no reciprocity to stay accurate.
K06 Data challenges REFUSE
AS IT WOULD LOOK

metric: RMSE on a held-out set · 88 teams · prize: 5,000 reputation
1team_gradientboost0.0412

THE CASE FOR
Challenges genuinely produce methods — CASP transformed structure prediction. They also drive a dataset's reuse count harder than anything else on this list.
THE INCENTIVE IT CREATES
Optimisation against a single held-out metric. Four decimal places apart at the top means the leader is fitting the test set, and the prose that says so gets no points.
THE NARROWER VERSION I WOULD ACCEPT
A challenge whose submission is a method card, judged on reproducibility by a panel, with the metric as one input among several. That is a different feature from K06 as written, and it should be specced as one — not slipped in under this name.

WHY THIS ONE IS DIFFERENT
The mechanic rewards replication, and replication is the goal — not a proxy for it. There is nothing to game, because a failed replication scores exactly as well as a successful one.
MY ERROR
I grouped this with data challenges because both say "challenge". They are opposites: one ranks people on a metric, the other pays people to check each other's work.
WHERE IT SLOTS IN
It sits directly on the method card as a fourth action beside Fork and Record a run, and it feeds the Replication axis and the reuse counter that already exist. Recommend for the next batch.
K22 Internal ethical gamification review ADOPT AS PROCESS
NOT A SCREEN · A GATE IN THE BUILD PROCESS
FOUR QUESTIONS, ASKED OF EVERY ENGAGEMENT FEATURE BEFORE IT IS SPECCED
01Does it reward the behaviour, or a proxy for the behaviour?
02What does a person optimising for this metric do that is bad science?
03Who does it disadvantage — and is that the junior, the slow, or the honest?
04If we removed it in two years, would users feel relieved or robbed?
Recorded per feature, next to its coverage row, so the reasoning survives the person who made the call.
MY ERROR, AGAIN
I counted K22 as a mechanic we refused. It is not a mechanic at all — it is the review that produced this board, and the Engagement_Mechanics tab is its first output.
WHY IT MATTERS COMMERCIALLY
It is a procurement answer. Camille's institution will ask how engagement decisions are governed; "we have a documented review with four criteria" is a better answer than a values statement.
RECOMMENDATION
Make it a standing item, run it quarterly against shipped features rather than only new ones, and publish the results. A mechanic that passed review at launch can turn harmful once the population changes.

No percentage. Each prompt states what it unlocks, measured in how many real searches would have found you.
THE PRINCIPLE
The workbook's mitigation is "weight completeness by fields that improve discovery, not by field count". Taken to its conclusion that means deleting the percentage entirely — a number that can reach 100% is a number people will farm. See it built.

THE DISTINCTION WORTH HOLDING
Batching is for the user; send-time optimisation is for the metric. They look like the same feature in a spec and are opposites in practice. K12 is marked Must-have, so it ships — with the half that serves the reader and without the half that serves the dashboard.
K20 Public commitments ALREADY BUILT · AS PRE-REG
THE HABIT-LOOP VERSION
"I will submit this paper by 30 November"
14 people watching12 days left
Public accountability for a date. Rewards announcing over doing, and publicly punishes the experiment that took longer than hoped — which is most of them.
WHAT SHIPPED · D2 SCREEN 18
FROZENPREREG-0044
A commitment to a hypothesis and an analysis plan, time-stamped and citable — not to a delivery date.
Nobody is watching a countdown. The commitment binds what you will conclude, not when you will finish.
SAME PSYCHOLOGY, BETTER OBJECT
Both use public commitment to change behaviour. Pre-registration aims it at the thing that actually corrupts results — changing the hypothesis after seeing the data — instead of at a deadline nobody controls.
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS three decisions for you, one correction from me
BUILD K07 NEXT
Replication challenges are the one mechanic on this list whose incentive points exactly where the product does. It needs one screen and it strengthens the differentiator.
ADOPT K22 AS A RITUAL
Four questions, quarterly, published. It is also the cleanest answer to the governance question every institutional buyer will ask.
HOLD THE THREE REFUSALS
K10, K11 and K06 all reward a proxy. They will be proposed again under different names — the mocks above are what to compare against.
