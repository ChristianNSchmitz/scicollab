SIGNING IS THE REVIEWER'S CHOICE
Chosen per review, never by policy, and changeable from anonymous to signed later but never the reverse. A junior reviewer criticising a powerful lab has a real reason to stay anonymous, and the design does not punish it.
A REVIEW IS AN OUTPUT
Its own DOI, citable, listed on the reviewer's profile and CV export, and counted on the Reviewing axis. Anonymous reviews still count — the profile shows the number, not the papers.

HOW YOU WERE FOUND · AND THE LIMITS
Matched on tag expertise, an accepted-answer record, and no co-authorship with any author in five years.
Rate-limited to two invitations a month per person, whoever is asking.
Not matched on h-index, seniority or institution — a postdoc who has answered fourteen questions on this exact problem is a better reviewer than a professor who has not.
WHY "MARKETPLACE" IS THE WRONG WORD
There is no payment and no bidding here. Paid review creates an incentive to accept work you cannot do well, and a market in reviews is a market in favourable reviews. What is exchanged is credit: a DOI, an axis contribution, and a line on your CV.

RIGHT OF REPLY
Authors are notified of every comment and their reply is always shown directly beneath it, marked AUTHOR. A criticism that stands unanswered because nobody told them is a failure of the platform, not of the author.
NO ANONYMOUS COMMENTS
Signed review is optional; signed commenting is not. An anonymous reviewer is invited into a structured process — an anonymous commenter on a published paper is a different thing entirely, and the failure mode is well documented.
MODERATION
Authors cannot delete comments. They can flag, and the institution's review queue decides in the open — the same three-approval mechanism as everywhere else.
