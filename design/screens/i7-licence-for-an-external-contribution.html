UNRESOLVED IN THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK The default licence for contributions to someone else's artifact is not yet decided by SciCollab. This screen presents both candidates neutrally so the founders and counsel can choose. Nothing below should be read as a recommendation.

CC0 1.0
public domain dedication · no conditions
You waive all rights in your contribution. Anyone may use it for anything, without attributing you.
ARGUMENTS FOR
No attribution stacking. A card with 40 contributors under CC BY accumulates 40 attribution obligations, and downstream users cannot comply in practice.
Legally simplest for reuse in commercial and clinical settings, where licence review is a real cost.
Matches what most data repositories now recommend for metadata and small factual contributions.
ARGUMENTS AGAINST
Contributors get no enforceable right to be named — and this platform's entire thesis is that credit for small contributions should be real.
Not accepted by some institutions' IP policies, which may prevent staff contributing at all.
Irreversible. A waiver cannot be withdrawn.
In practice attribution would still be displayed by the platform — but as a convention we could change, not a right the contributor holds.
CC BY 4.0
attribution required · everything else permitted
You keep copyright. Anyone may use your contribution for any purpose provided they credit you.
ARGUMENTS FOR
Credit becomes a right rather than a courtesy, which is consistent with everything else the product does about attribution.
Familiar to researchers, accepted by essentially every institutional IP policy and funder mandate.
The contributor keeps standing to object if their work is misused.
ARGUMENTS AGAINST
Attribution stacking is a genuine problem at scale, and a widely forked card could become impractical to reuse compliantly.
Licence compatibility questions when a card mixes CC BY contributions with CC0 or proprietary material.
Creates a real, if small, legal risk for downstream commercial users — which may suppress exactly the reuse we want.
A middle path exists — CC BY with a platform-level collective attribution notice — but it is untested and would need counsel's view.

Shown at the moment of contribution, not buried in account settings, because this is the only moment the person is thinking about this specific piece of work.
