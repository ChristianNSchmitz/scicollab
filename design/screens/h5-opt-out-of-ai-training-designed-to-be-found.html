Your work is not training data unless you say so.

The default for every account is off. We did not bury this, pre-tick it, or phrase it so the "generous" option is the confusing one. If SciCollab is going to ask researchers to put unpublished work in one place, this is the setting that decides whether they do.
Use my public artifacts to improve retrieval and suggestions
Affects search ranking, tag suggestions and the retrieval index. Your public work is already readable; this decides whether it is learned from.
OFF
Use my private and lab-visibility work
Not available. There is no toggle here because there is no circumstance in which we will do this — not with consent, not anonymised, not aggregated. Unpublished work is the thing researchers are most afraid of losing control of, and the only credible answer is "never".
NEVER
Allow third-party AI providers to access my public artifacts
Separate from the above, and separately off. Includes API access by external model providers and inclusion in any licensed corpus.
OFF
CHANGING YOUR MIND IS REAL, NOT COSMETIC
Turning any of these off removes your content from the next index build, within 30 days, and we tell you the date it completed. Where content has already contributed to a trained model we say so plainly rather than implying it can be unlearned.
WHY THIS SCREEN GETS PROMINENCE
Camille's evaluation criteria are governance, audit logs and SSO readiness. In every institutional procurement conversation from 2024 onward, "what happens to our data" is the question that ends the deal. A buried opt-out reads as a plan to change the default later.
WHERE IT APPEARS
First item in Settings — above notifications, above the profile
A link in the header of every AI panel in the product
Once during onboarding, as a screen you must look at
In the institution's governance console as an org-wide setting
THE HONEST COST
Defaulting to off means a materially worse retrieval index at launch. That is the correct trade: a platform that opts researchers in by default gets one good quarter and then a decade of being the tool nobody's legal office will approve.
