PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY · 148 WORDS
Cells grown in the laboratory are used to test new gene-editing tools. We found that these cells stop responding to one common editing method after they have been grown and split about eighteen times — the success rate falls from roughly four in ten to fewer than one in twenty, and it does not recover. We tested whether poor-quality chemicals were to blame and they were not. Laboratories that keep using older cells may be repeating experiments that cannot work, without knowing why. Our practical recommendation is to freeze a large stock of young cells at the start of a project and return to it, rather than continuing to grow the same batch.
