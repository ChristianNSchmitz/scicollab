READ ROW 7 — THAT IS THE WHOLE DESIGN
The most accurate submission sits seventh. It wins on the metric and loses on everything that makes a result usable: nobody has reproduced it, there is no protocol, and it was only ever tested on one line. Under a Kaggle-style board it would be first, and the field would learn nothing from it.
FOUR AXES, EQUALLY WEIGHTED
Accuracy — against a held-out set, as usual
Reproduced — how many independent labs ran your card and got your number
Documented — conditions and failure range stated
Generalises — how many systems it was tested on
Three of the four cannot be optimised alone — they require other people to act, which is what makes them expensive to game.
WHAT THE FIELD GAINS
CASP-style challenges genuinely moved structure prediction. The failure mode was always that the winning method was a black box nobody could run. Requiring the submission to be a method card fixes exactly that, and leaves 31 runnable protocols behind when the challenge closes.
PRIZE
Credit and a citable challenge record — no reputation transfer, no cash. The workbook's caution about wealthy labs buying attention applies to bounties; here there is nothing to buy.
